ABA Techniques Observed

Discrete Trial Training (DTT)

The adult structures the session into short trials: cue (“Vamos deslizar até o próximo cone”), learner response (skating toward the target), and consequence (praise or corrective prompt). Data is recorded on a clipboard after each attempt, allowing precise measurement of stride length and cone navigation accuracy.

Prompting Hierarchy

Prompts fade from physical (light elbow guidance) to gestural (pointing at the cone) and finally to verbal (“Lembre do joelho flexionado”). The adult waits several seconds before escalating prompts, reinforcing independent initiation whenever possible.

Differential Reinforcement

Specific praise (“Bom equilíbrio!”) and tangible reinforcers (sticker placed on the schedule) are delivered contingent on fluent strides. When the girl hesitates, the adult withholds the sticker but maintains neutral affect, ensuring reinforcement is tied to target behavior.

Verbal Behavior Categorization

The learner’s utterances function as mands (“Posso pausar um momento?”) and tacts (“Estou deslizando mais rápido!”). The adult models intraverbals to expand conversational reciprocity (“O que vem depois do cone azul?”), prompting descriptive responses.

Behavioral Analytics

Skill Acquisition

Successive approximations show increased stride symmetry. Latency between cue and initiation decreases from 4 seconds to 1.5 seconds across trials, indicating strong response to prompt fading.

Motivating Operations

The visual schedule with earned stickers functions as a conditioned reinforcer. Break requests occur after 4 continuous trials, suggesting the need for scheduled sensory regulation intervals to maintain motivation.

Generalization

The adult rotates cone colors between trials to probe for generalization. The learner successfully follows altered color patterns, demonstrating concept transfer of “next obstacle” beyond fixed stimuli.

Recommendations for Practitioners

  • Maintain differential reinforcement with descriptive praise to support fluent gait while gradually thinning tangible rewards.
  • Embed intraverbal exchanges after each cone to solidify sequencing and encourage narrative language about movement.
  • Introduce peer modeling in future sessions to expand social referencing while preserving safety supports.
  • Track duration of independent glides to quantify endurance gains alongside accuracy.
